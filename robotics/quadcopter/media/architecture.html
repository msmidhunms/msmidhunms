FIG 1 · DEPLOYMENT

Two containers, one browser

Everything ROS and Gazebo lives in the drone_sim container. Your browser is the only thing outside it: one tab is the ground station, the other is the VNC desktop where Gazebo and RViz draw. A USB gamepad reaches the container through the browser, because Docker on macOS cannot pass USB devices through.

FIG 2 · ROS 2 GRAPH

Who publishes what

flight_controller is the only node that commands the motors. Pilot and autonomy reach it through two different inputs, so it always knows which source it is following. Everything else listens to one status topic.

FIG 3 · CONTROL LOOP

From reference to rotor speeds

A geometric tracking controller on SE(3). The outer loop turns position error into a desired acceleration, that acceleration fixes the thrust direction and therefore the desired attitude, and the inner loop drives the attitude error to zero on the rotation group itself, with no Euler angles and no gimbal lock. Both loops run once per odometry message, so control stays locked to simulation time even when Gazebo runs slower than real time.

FIG 4 · REFERENCE GENERATION

Two ways to say where to fly

The controller only ever sees one kind of input: a smooth reference with position, velocity, acceleration and heading. Manual flying and missions are two different ways of producing it.

FIG 5 · FLIGHT STATES

The commander's state machine

Every button, service call and failsafe is a transition on this graph. Modes only exist while flying; landing always hands control back to MANUAL.

FIG 6 · VIDEO PIPELINE

From a flight log to a film

Gazebo's live view is for flying. The cinematic video is rendered afterwards from the flight log, so it can take as long as it needs per frame without slowing the simulation.

WHERE THINGS LIVE

Repository map

  • drone_description/vehicle.yaml, the xacro SDF model generated from it, and the airfield world (generated by gen_world.py).
  • drone_control/…/controller.pyGeometric SE(3) controller (Fig 3). With mixer.py, commander.py and trajectory.py it has no ROS dependency.
  • drone_control/…/sim.pyRigid-body simulator with Gazebo's rotor model, used by the unit tests to tune and check the controller in seconds.
  • drone_control/…/*_node.pyThin ROS 2 wrappers: flight_controller, teleop, mission_manager, visualizer, flight_recorder, web_ui, demo_flight.
  • drone_control/…/web/Ground station page (virtual sticks, keyboard, Gamepad API, telemetry).
  • drone_bringup/sim.launch.py, bridge, gains, joystick mapping, missions, RViz layout.
  • render/render_flight.pyBlender renderer (Fig 6).
  • docker/Dockerfile with sim and render targets, supervisord, VNC desktop.
